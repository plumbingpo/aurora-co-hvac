Keeping Your Colorado Springs HVAC System Running Longer

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

How old is your system? Its age says a lot about what comes next. With regular service, most air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, gas furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that point and also shows rising bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or frequent repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement when the first heat wave hits.

Small problems don't stay minor for long. Take a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a miserable night.

Typical Heating & Cooling Issues in Colorado Springs

HVAC systems in this area face a familiar set of enemies. The area's older housing stock adds its own layer, undersized returns, leaky or crushed ductwork, and additions that no duct run ever reached.
